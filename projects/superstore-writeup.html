01

Business Problem

Superstore retailers routinely discount orders to win sales, but not every discount pays for itself. Without breaking profitability down by discount level, category, or region, a store can hit healthy total sales while quietly bleeding margin on a subset of orders — and nobody notices until the year-end numbers come in soft.

02

Stakeholder Request

The Operations Manager asked for an analysis that went beyond total sales and profit: specifically, which discount levels were actually eating into margin, and which categories and regions were carrying the business versus dragging it down — so pricing and discount policy could be adjusted with evidence rather than gut feel.

03

Business Objectives

  • Quantify total sales, profit, and overall margin across the full order base (9,994 records).
  • Break down sales and profit by product category and region to identify top and bottom performers.
  • Analyse profitability across discount bands to find the point where discounting stops paying off.
  • Package the findings into an interactive dashboard stakeholders can filter by Region, Category, Discount Range, and Order Date without needing to open Power BI or write SQL.
04

Dataset Overview

Source
Sample Superstore dataset (Kaggle)

Star schema — DateTable, a Measures table (Total Sales, Total Profit, Profit Margin %, Total Orders, Avg Discount %, Loss-Making Orders, Average Order Value), and the Sample_Superstore fact table
05

Data Cleaning

The raw Superstore CSV had a character encoding issue — one column wasn't reading correctly in its original UTF-8 format, which corrupted values on load. The fix was to open the file in VS Code, identify the affected column, and re-save it with the correct encoding, resolving the corrupted values before the data was loaded into the analysis.

06

Analysis Process

Built eight core DAX measures on top of the star schema — continued practice for DAX as part of ongoing learning — combining Power BI and MySQL to calculate sales, profit, margin, and discount-impact metrics. Orders were grouped into discount bands (0%, 1–20%, 21–40%, 41%+) to isolate the point where profitability breaks down.

08

Key Insights

1

Category Performance

Technology generated the highest sales (R836,154.06) and the highest profit (R145,455.31). Furniture had the lowest profit (R18,451.18) despite comparable sales volume to Office Supplies.

2

Regional Performance

West recorded the highest sales (R725,457.83) and the strongest profit margin (14.94%). South had the lowest sales (R391,721.83), while Central had the weakest margin (7.92%).

3

Discount Impact

Profitability inverts sharply as discounting increases — average profit per order turns negative from the 21–40% band (–R77.86) and worsens at 41%+ (–R106.71).

4

Overall Margin

R2,297,200.64 in total sales converts to just R286,396.62 in profit — a 12.47% blended margin, dragged down almost entirely by the deep-discount segment.

09

Business Impact

The core finding — that orders discounted above 20% average a negative profit per order — means a meaningful share of the order base isn't just low-margin, it's actively losing money on every sale. The 460 orders in the 21–40% band and 933 orders in the 41%+ band (1,393 orders combined, roughly 14% of all orders) together account for an estimated loss of around R135,376, currently being absorbed by the healthier 0% and 1–20% segments.

≈ R135,376
Estimated annual loss from orders discounted above 20% — recoverable by capping discount policy

Capping discounts at 20% wouldn't just stop the bleeding — it would let that recovered margin drop straight back into overall profit, lifting the 12.47% blended margin without needing a single extra sale. The one risk to weigh: if the 21%+ tier is where price-sensitive customers convert, a hard cap could cost some of those 1,393 orders outright, which is why a phased rollout is worth testing before applying it store-wide.

10

Recommendations

  • Cap discounts at 20%, where average profit per order turns negative — the single highest-leverage fix, worth an estimated R135k in recovered margin.
  • Pilot the cap in one or two regions or categories first to confirm it doesn't suppress order volume before rolling out company-wide.
  • Investigate why Furniture underperforms on profit despite solid sales — a candidate for its own discount policy review, separate from the general cap.
  • Use Central's weak 7.92% margin, despite mid-tier sales, as a starting point for a regional pricing audit.
