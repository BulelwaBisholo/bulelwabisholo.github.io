01

Business Problem

The Superstore business needs a clearer understanding of what's actually driving profitability, not just sales volume. Discounts are applied broadly across products and orders, but it isn't clear whether they're growing the business or quietly eroding margins. This analysis examines 9,692 transactions across 4,931 orders to uncover how sales, discounts, and profit interact.

02

Stakeholder Request

"Our sales numbers look healthy, but profit isn't keeping pace. We need to understand whether our discounting strategy is helping or hurting us, and where we should focus to protect margins." — Head of Sales (simulated stakeholder brief)

03

Business Objectives

  • Identify which categories, products, and customers drive the highest sales and profit.
  • Objective 2 — e.g. segment applicants by risk level.
  • Objective 3 — e.g. build a dashboard the credit team can use without SQL knowledge.
04

Dataset Overview

Source Kaggle / Public Superstore dataset
Rows 9,692
Unique orders 4,931
Columns ~20
Format CSV / relational tables

Each row represents a single order line item, including product, category, customer, segment, region, sales value, discount applied, and profit. The dataset was provided in a clean, analysis-ready format with no missing-value or structural issues.

05

Data Cleaning

The dataset was received in a clean, analysis-ready state. Initial checks confirmed row counts, unique orders, and distinct category values before moving into analysis — no additional cleaning steps were required.

06

Analysis Process

1

Exploratory data analysis

Counted total rows, identified unique orders, and reviewed distinct categories and value ranges to understand the dataset's shape.

2

Feature engineering & segmentation

Compared sales, profit, and order volume across discount levels using HAVING and aggregate functions to isolate the discount-profit relationship.

3

Validation

Describe how findings were checked or validated before being reported.

Dashboard & Visualisations

Power BI dashboard in progress — coming soon.

08

Key Insights

Insight 01

A 0% discount generated the highest overall sales and profit across the dataset.

Insight 02

0% and 20% discount levels were the strongest performers for both sales and profitability.

Insight 03

Higher discount levels were consistently associated with weaker profitability — more discount didn't mean more profit.

09

Business Impact

These findings show the current discounting approach may be working against profitability in several segments. Left unchecked, broad high-discount strategies risk growing sales on paper while quietly shrinking margins — a pattern that's easy to miss without breaking discount levels out from overall performance.

10

Recommendations

01

Review discount levels above 20%, which show a consistent link to weaker profit performance.

02

Prioritise profitable sales, not just volume — factor profit margin into how sales performance is judged.

03

Target discounts more precisely, applying them to specific products, customers, or campaigns rather than broadly.
