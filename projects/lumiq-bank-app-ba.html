LUMIQ Bank App

Please note: LUMIQ Bank is fictional. This is a simulated business analysis case study. The scenarios, stakeholders, targets and examples are illustrative, and no real customer research or bank data was used.
01

Business Problem

Customers of the fictional LUMIQ Bank App may not know whether a payment is processing, successful, failed or awaiting investigation. When the status is unclear or delayed, they may contact support, try the payment again, or lose confidence in the service.

Business impact to investigate

  • Avoidable customer-support contacts about payment status.
  • Possible duplicate payments when customers can't tell if the first attempt worked.
  • Longer investigations when cases lack consistent status information or ownership.
  • Lower customer confidence, and possible complaints or attrition.

Opportunity: show the reliable status information the payment systems already hold, define clear exception handling and ownership, and keep customers updated until the payment is resolved.

03

Objectives & Scope

  • Give customers a clear, accurate status and timestamp for each payment.
  • Never show a successful outcome before success is confirmed.
  • Reduce avoidable status-related support contacts and repeat-payment attempts.
  • Create a consistent escalation and customer-update process for unresolved payments.
  • Give Operations measurable service indicators.
In scopeOut of scope

A five-minute target was discussed as a service aspiration for the exception journey. It needs validation with Payments Operations and technology teams and is not a guaranteed settlement time.

04

AS-IS Process

The assumed current state: a customer pays, downstream systems process it, but the app's confirmation is delayed or unclear. The customer contacts support, support asks Payments Operations to investigate, and the customer waits.

Key gaps to address

  • No consistently visible status lifecycle for the customer.
  • No clear escalation deadline or service-level expectation.
  • No guaranteed proactive communication during investigation.
  • No explicit feedback loop after escalation.
  • Risk of unsafe retries when the first attempt's outcome is unknown.

The current-state process and gaps are hypotheses to validate, not verified facts about a real bank.

05

TO-BE Process

The proposed future state uses the transaction record as the source for the customer's status view. It separates processing, pending, successful and failed outcomes, never shows success before authoritative confirmation, and gives unresolved payments a controlled exception journey.

ScenarioProposed behaviour
ProcessingShow Processing or Pending with timestamp and reference; never Successful
Confirmed successShow Transaction successful; proof of payment available afterwards
FailedShow Failed with a plain-language reason where safe, and a clear next step
UnresolvedRoute to Payments Operations with an owner and a validated handling target
RetryBounded retries with idempotency controls to prevent duplicates
Funds need correctionReturn or release funds to the original method where applicable, without a customer claim

Design principles: accurate status before reassurance, no false success, no duplicate transaction on retry, clear ownership, customer updates, auditability and minimal customer effort.

Worked example: acceptance criteria for US-01

Acceptance criteria turn a story into something testable. These are the criteria for the first story, and they feed directly into UAT-01 and UAT-02.

  • Given a payment is processing, when I open its details, then the status displays Processing or Pending.
  • Given authoritative confirmation of success is received, when the record refreshes, then the status displays Transaction successful.
  • The status must never display Successful before confirmation.

09

RAID & KPIs

RAID highlights

  • Risk: status data may be delayed or inconsistent across payment services, so the customer sees the wrong status.
  • Risk: retry logic could create duplicate payments, so use idempotency controls and bounded retries.
  • Assumption: a reliable transaction reference and status history are available or can be exposed.
  • Dependency: payment service/API capability and notification infrastructure.

Success measures

Status-related contact rate, payment exception rate, escalation rate, time to resolution, proactive update coverage, duplicate payment incident rate and customer satisfaction after resolution.

No baseline values or improvement percentages are claimed. Baselines come first, then targets agreed with the business owner.

10

Recommendations

01

Validate the current payment lifecycle and the authoritative status source with Payments Operations and Engineering.

02

Agree a controlled status vocabulary and transition rules, including timeouts and ambiguous outcomes.

03

Prioritise status accuracy and transaction-detail visibility before advanced notification features.

04

Design retry and duplicate-prevention controls with technical and risk stakeholders.

05

Prototype the transaction-detail experience, test it with customers and support staff, then run UAT on normal, failed, delayed, duplicate-attempt and recovery scenarios.
